SACRAMENTO, Calif. — November 24, 2026 — The election reform campaign California Approves is building support for an initiative that would let voters back multiple candidates in elections after the California Attorney General issued the official title and summary for the measure on November 17. The campaign has until May 17, 2027, to collect 546,651 valid signatures to qualify the measure for the November 2028 ballot.

The initiative would give Californians more choice when voting for congressional and state offices in primary, special, and recall elections. Instead of marking a single candidate, voters could mark as many candidates as they wish, a method known as approval voting. In primaries, the two candidates with the most votes would advance to the general election, as they do today. General elections would not change. Special elections for the U.S. House and state legislative vacancies would be decided in a single round, as recall elections are today. The official fiscal estimate projects local, and potentially state, savings of up to several million dollars per election from eliminating some special elections, and one-time implementation costs in the millions, potentially exceeding $10 million.

“Giving Californians the freedom to vote for one or more candidates means they can support everyone whose values align with their own,” said Amanda Mahlendorf, the initiative’s proponent. “And candidates can earn support from any voter, even one who also likes someone else. That’s how we elect officeholders who are focused on the things Californians agree on, because winning requires earning the broadest approval, not just a loyal base.”

California Approves is now preparing to gather signatures. Voters can pledge support for the measure at californiaapproves.org/our-reforms/approval-primary/#pledge and will be notified when the official petitions are ready to sign. Groups interested in supporting approval voting at a statewide or local level, including clubs, unions, advocacy groups, and elected officials, can contact Alan Savage at alan@californiaapproves.org.

“If you think elections should be won by the person with the most supporters, then this is for you,” said Alan Savage, founder of California Approves.

Why approval voting?

Approval voting is a system where voters may vote for as many candidates as they support, and the candidates with the most votes win. The election method is designed to eliminate vote-splitting between multiple candidates with overlapping supporters.

“By giving voters the freedom to approve every candidate they want, all support can be added up, revealing how much overall support each candidate truly has,” Mahlendorf said. “Giving voters more freedom creates fair competition for broad support and delivers better representation.”
